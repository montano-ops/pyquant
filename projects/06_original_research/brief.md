# Capstone 6 — Original Research (Your Question)

**The paper:** yours. This is the thesis. Start only after module 13
(validation) — starting earlier selects for people who fool themselves
well.

## The contract

1. **A question nobody in the course answered for you.** Narrow beats
   grand: "does the earnings-drift anomaly survive in sector ETFs" is a
   project; "can I predict the market" is a diagnosis.
2. **Pre-registration, formally.** 01_question.ipynb completed and
   dated: H₀/H₁, data, windows, the ONE headline test (name the
   statistic and its threshold NOW), the robustness set, and the
   stopping rule. After that, changes go in the log — visibly.
3. **A bias audit as a designed experiment**, not a paragraph: your
   design's top three biases, each with a test that could catch it, run
   and reported (even when — especially when — they pass).
4. **The full standards stack**: data audit (module 05), honest SEs
   (modules 02–04, 06, 09), multiple-testing control (module 13 —
   deflated Sharpe or an equivalent you can defend), costs and capacity
   (module 12), and the three-way verdict.
5. **A null result is a success** if it is *tight*: "X does not predict
   Y: the 95% CI on the spread is [−2bp, +3bp]" is publishable-grade
   work. A loose maybe is not.

## Suggested shapes (not a menu — a prompt)

Replication-with-a-twist (take a capstone 1–5 design to a new universe,
frequency, or decade and test whether it transfers); an anomaly
autopsy (pick a published effect and run its multiple-testing/
post-publication-decay gauntlet); a data-forensics project (survivorship
or microstructure bias, quantified with controlled simulations).

## Deliverables

Nine notebooks + the report + a one-page **defense memo**: the three
hardest questions a skeptic would ask, and your answers, with numbers.

## The real grade

Years from now you will not remember your Sharpe. You will remember
whether you can be trusted alone with data. That is what this project
certifies — and its rubric scores exactly that.
