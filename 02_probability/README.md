# Module 02 — Probability (Weeks 3–5)

The learner's weakest area gets the deepest treatment. Every concept is
welded to returns, portfolios, or trading outcomes — there is no dice-and-
cardigan probability here (except where a die *is* the cleanest model of a
trading day).

**The through-line of this module:** thinking in *distributions* — not "what
happened" but "what are the possible outcomes and their likelihoods". The
module ends with the two ideas that power all of statistics: the law of large
numbers (averages converge — slowly) and the central limit theorem (averages
are approximately normal — with caveats you will test against real data).

| Week | Days | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1–7 | Random experiments → probability rules → conditioning/Bayes → random variables → expectation & variance; **mini-project: the expectancy simulator** |
| 4 | 8–14 | Covariance & correlation → joint distributions & 2-asset portfolios → Bernoulli/binomial (win rates) → normal distribution → law of large numbers; **mini-project: correlation study** |
| 5 | 15–21 | Central limit theorem → random walks & efficiency → law of small numbers → Monte Carlo & bootstrap preview → why you can't shuffle time series; **checkpoint: is a 55% win rate luck?** |
