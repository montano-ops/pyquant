# Day 7 — Mini-Project: Beta Lab

**The deliverable:** a beta report on 10 names — the table (β̂, SE, t, R²,
α̂/yr), a rolling-beta chart, a stability analysis, and a bias audit —
generated by code, regenerable end-to-end. This is the research pattern in
miniature: question → data → method → result → interpretation → *how could
this be wrong?*

## Why beta deserves a whole day

Module 07 prices assets with beta; modules 11–12 hedge with it; desks quote
it daily. Yet "the beta of a stock" is a *regression output*, not a
constant of nature: it depends on the sample, the frequency, the
benchmark, and the window — and its sampling error (day 3) is routinely
larger than the differences people trade on. Today's lab is your chance to
feel all four dependencies with your own code, on ten names at once, before
module 07 builds theory on top of numbers you already distrust properly.

## The build

1. **Universe & data.** Real mode: 10 names from `research50` + SPY,
   2015 → today, log returns (adjusted prices — module 05's discipline:
   say why adjusted, in one line, in the report). Synthetic mode: an
   11-asset one-factor world (`qrc.synth`), which *plants* betas via the
   correlation/vol structure — treat recovery of relative beta ordering as
   your unit test.
2. **The table.** Per name: full-sample β̂, classical SE, White SE, t
   (White), α̂ %/yr with SE, R². Sorted by β̂. One anomaly scan: which
   names' White-vs-classical SE ratio departs from 1 by > 20%?
3. **Rolling betas.** 252-day rolling β̂ per name (a loop is honest; write
   it first, vectorize later if you must). Chart all ten on one panel —
   then add the ±2 SE band for ONE name (ten bands is wallpaper).
4. **Stability analysis.** (a) Range of each name's rolling β̂ (5th–95th
   percentile) vs its full-sample SE — how much of the wiggle is sampling
   noise vs time variation? (b) Persistence: cross-sectional corr of
   betas year-over-year (β̂ ranking now vs ranking next year) — the number
   that makes beta-usability a measurement, not a slogan. (c) Optional:
   compare with the industry "adjusted beta" = ⅔β̂ + ⅓·1 — whose failure
   mode does that shrinkage answer (module 03's shrinkage discipline)?
5. **The bias audit.** One tight paragraph: benchmark choice, window,
   adjustment method, universe (research50 is today's survivors — say it),
   and what each choice could do to the table. End with the sentence
   beginning "These betas cannot be used for…".

## Method notes

- Adjusted closes, log returns, aligned dates from the panel (outer join,
  then dropna per-pair is fine at 15 years of megacaps; state what you did).
- The report is **code**: the table is a printed DataFrame, the audit is a
  cell, and re-running the notebook regenerates the entire deliverable on
  new data.
- Synthetic mode is a unit test, not an escape hatch: if your machinery
  can't rank planted betas correctly, it has no business near real data.
- Keep the SE flavor discussion honest per name: the ratio column is the
  audit, not the default choice.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Criterion | Bar |
|---|---|
| Table | All columns present; White SE actually computed; sorted; interpretable in one read |
| Rolling chart | 10 lines, one SE band, dated axis; readable without zoom |
| Stability | The 5–95% range numbers AND the YoY corr reported, each with one sentence that says what they mean for beta-as-input |
| Audit | Four choices named with direction of effect; the "cannot be used for" sentence present |
| Regenerability | `QRC_DATA=synthetic` run passes end-to-end (the grader runs exactly this) |
